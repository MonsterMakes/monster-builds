SteelHSS ASTM A500 Gr. C; plate A36; E70XX
WoodThermally modified ash, oiled to hold its colour. The steel frame is the structure; the planks are a surface.

Hold points

  1. Finish grade and pier. M4 bears directly on grade over the existing pier, on self-adhered flashing. M4 is not shimmed. Measure its pitch along the stair and cross slope across it as set, and cut the M1 level cut to match, as a compound cut where there is cross slope. Measure the wall face the same way for the plumb cut.
  2. Rod positions. Measured at 3/4" and 5-1/4" from the first riser face, 5-1/4" apart across, 1/2" diameter. Re-verify before drilling; both rows land near the downhill end of M4, so the plate runs 1-1/2" past the first riser; the 1-1/4" round washers clear the M2b stub walls.
  3. Wall anchors. 6" stem with #4 verticals at 12" o.c., 1-3/4" clear at the back face, so bars sit about 4" from the front face. Scan and lay out anchors between bars. Embedment 3-1/2" minimum to 4-1/2" maximum in the 6" stem, 9/16" hole.
  4. Guard sequence. The posts are propped by the top rail. The rail must be installed, spliced over posts with its internal splines, and anchored at the wall before any cable is tensioned. Tensioning against an unbraced post makes it a cantilever and overloads the tread mounting.
  5. Propped post assumption. The guard posts are propped by the continuous top rail, so the stem welds see about 1,200 in-lb of moment and 1,244 lb of shear at the bottom post, carried into the side walls of the 3 in end tube. The engineer should confirm the stem-to-tube weld and the collar connection for that case.
  6. Engineer of record review. A cantilevered mono stringer is not a prescriptive IRC stair. Expect stamped calcs and possibly special inspection of the adhesive anchors.

Fabrication sequence

  1. Verify the hold points on S-001. Confirm the bearing plane at the pier and locate the rods and the wall reinforcing.
  2. Tap the guard posts for the handrail brackets: (2) 1/4-20 in the inboard face of each stair post, left side ascending, at 4" centres about 34" above the nosing line. Plug with stainless button heads until the handrail is fitted. This cannot be added after coating without drilling finished steel.
  3. Cut M1. Lay out the six stub centrelines along the top face from the long point. Drill a 3/16" hole 1-3/8" downhill of each, before the stubs go on.
  4. Build a slope jig at 31.43°. Fit and weld the M2 stubs, checking every one square, level and on the nosing line. Build the seven M7 tread frames on a flat jig and check both diagonals. Weld the M8 collars between the front and back members, then the M3 plate between the collars on 1/16" shims.
  5. Weld M6. Tack M4 and the M2b stub.
  6. Dry fit. Trim the M1 level cut to suit the actual bearing plane. Drill the 9/16" rod holes through M4 to the rods as set. Drill wall anchors through M6 as a template. Drop each tread frame onto its stub, with the neoprene pad in place, and drill the through-bolt holes through collars and stub together: 13/32 round in the stub, slotted 13/32 × 9/16 in the collars. At the three post treads, notch both planks at each end around the post, 3-1/8 along the plank, 1-7/16 deep on the front plank and 1-1/2 on the back, and seal the end grain.
  7. Remove, finish all welds, drill vents, grind, deburr. Blast and powder coat; cold-galvanize any interior surfaces that the coating will not reach.
  8. Install the stair: flashing under M4, set and torque the rods, install wall anchors per the ICC-ES cure time, set the stub pads, drop the tread frames on, fit the through bolts, then screw the ash from below.
  9. Install the guard: stand the posts on their stems and through-bolt, then the top rail with its splines and the wall connection. Tension cables last.
S-603

Finish schedule

Not to scale; dimensions govern

ItemRequirement
Surface preparationSSPC-SP10 near-white blast, by the coater. Do not accept SP6 commercial blast. Grind all welds smooth and break every sharp edge before blasting; blasting will not fix a sharp arris or a weld crater.
Coating systemZinc-rich powder primer, then super-durable polyester (TGIC) topcoat. Tell the coater: exterior, high UV at 4,500 ft, freeze-thaw, snowmelt at grade. Do not hot-dip galvanize. The stair is a single precision weldment; an 840°F kettle dip will distort the stringer and take the seven saddles out of plane, and it cannot be straightened once coated.
Before it goes to the coaterNo oil, grease, cutting fluid, layout dye or wax crayon on any face that stays. Blasting drives oil in rather than removing it. Mark with soapstone or on offcuts only.
Storage between dry fit and coatingLight flash rust is removed by the blast and is not a concern. Pitting is. Store off the ground on blocks, sloped to drain, either open or fully covered. Never leave a tarp lying on the steel; it traps condensation.
Vents and drains, all before coating3/16" hole in the stringer top face under each M2 stub, its downhill edge flush with the inside of the stub's downhill wall, so each stub drains and vents into the stringer. 3/16" weep notch in the bottom edge of M1 at its lowest corner on M4, as set in the field. 3/16" weep hole through the side wall of M2b at its lowest corner, centred 3/8" above M4 so it clears the 1/4" fillet. No other vents in M1 or the stubs; the stubs vent through their open tops. 3/8" in every guard post near its base. Any closed section that is welded shut and not vented can blow a weld in the cure oven.
After coatingTape over the stub holes, the M1 weep notch and the M2b weep hole while blasting so media stays out of the stringer; pull the tape before coating so they vent in the oven. After coating, leave all of them open. Water that gets past a stub pad runs down the stringer and out the notch onto M4.
Drilled and tapped holesAll drilling and tapping happens at dry fit, before coating: rod holes in M4, wall anchors through M6, through-bolt holes in the collars and stubs, plank screw holes in the tread frames, rail-to-plate taps, guard post cable holes at the rake angle, and the handrail bracket taps. Tapping through powder coat leaves bare threads in a wet location. Plug unused tapped holes with stainless button-head screws and sealant.
Dissimilar metalsType 316 stainless fasteners against coated steel. Use EPDM-bonded or nylon washers at every stainless-to-steel contact so the coating is not cut and the couple is broken.
Interiors the powder cannot reachElectrostatic powder does not throw down a long tube. Anything more than an inch or two inside a rail or post will be bare. Coat splines and reachable interior surfaces with a cold galvanizing compound of 90%+ zinc in the dry film (ZRC or Rust-Oleum Cold Galvanizing Compound), two coats. Hardware-store "galvanized look" paint is not sacrificial and does nothing.
FastenersType 316 throughout. Button or pan head, not countersunk, wherever the host material is 1/8" or thinner; countersinking a 1/8" wall leaves almost no bearing and the head pulls through. Bonded EPDM sealing washer under every head that passes through an exterior surface. Anti-seize on all stainless-to-stainless threads; 316 galls badly when dry.
Drains3/16" hole in the bottom wall within 1" of the low end of every rail and hollow post, in addition to the coating vents. Every closed section in this job gets water in it eventually; the only question is whether it can leave.
Touch-upKeep a matched aerosol or brush-grade epoxy from the coater for field damage. Any bare steel left in contact with wood or concrete will bleed rust through the finish within a season.
TreadsThermally modified ash: surface four sides, sand 120 grit, ease edges 1/8", seal ends. Finish all faces before install with a penetrating oil that carries UV pigment (Cutek, Penofin or a hardwax oil) to hold the brown. Recoat every 2–3 years. Left unfinished it silvers without harm, but colour is the reason for the finish.

Not to scale; dimensions govern

Design basis

Codes2018 IRC R312 and R311.7.8; IBC referenced for structural design and special inspection
Guard height36" above the walking surface, 36" above the nosing line on flights
HandrailRequired on flights of 4+ risers. A 2x3 cap rail is not graspable; a separate 1-1/2 square rail is provided
Infill10 cables, 4" sphere where the run is a guard

Hold points

  1. Cables last. The rail must be installed, spliced over posts with its splines, and anchored at both ends of every run before any cable is tensioned. Tensioning against an unbraced post makes it a cantilever carrying roughly 45,000 in-lb, which no spigot here will hold. Cables come off first for any later work.
  2. Master bath run tension. The master bath landing and stair guard run is tensioned to about 100 lb per cable, half the 200 lb used elsewhere. Its spans are about half as long, so it needs half the tension to pass the 4 in sphere, and the lower tension limits the one-sided pull on the master bath stair tread 1.
  3. Raked run. The 16 ft 6 in run along the planter climbs 15-1/2 in at about 4.5 degrees, rail parallel to the walk. Posts are plumb and one length with top plates cut at the rake. The survey confirms the exact rise and angle.
  4. Guard extent. Guard from the stair opening to the 110 degree corner. Everything past that corner is garden fence: same parts, no code obligation, cable tension reduced to snug.
  5. Post schedule is provisional pending the field survey on S-002 of the stair set.
  6. Engineer review of the wall spigots at reduced embedment in a 6 in stem, the cable tension load cases, and the stem-to-end-tube weld at the tread posts.
